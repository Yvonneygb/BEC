# BEC Daily | Vendor Negotiation

## Dialogue

**A (David Kim — Procurement Manager, Apex Manufacturing)**
**B (Rachel Torres — Senior Account Executive, GlobalPack Solutions)**

**A:** Rachel, thank you for coming in. We've been reviewing our packaging supply chain, and frankly, the fluctuation in your lead times over the past two quarters has been causing us some real scheduling headaches on the production floor.

**B:** I appreciate you flagging this directly, David. I won't make excuses — the Q1 delays were driven by raw material shortages at our upstream suppliers. However, we've since diversified our sourcing to include two backup mills, and our on-time delivery rate has climbed back to 94% in the last six weeks.

**A:** That's encouraging, but 94% still leaves a gap. Our production runs on a just-in-time model — any delay of more than 48 hours means we're shutting down a line. I need a firm commitment on a 48-hour delivery window with penalty clauses for breach.

**B:** I can commit to a 48-hour window for our standard SKUs, but the custom-printed cartons require three working days due to the setup time on the press. On penalties — our standard contract caps liquidated damages at 5% of the invoice value. For a volume account like yours, I can increase that to 8%, which is the maximum our legal team will entertain.

**A:** Eight percent is a start, but I'm more interested in preventing the breach than collecting damages. What about holding a buffer stock? If you maintain a two-week safety stock of our top twenty SKUs at your warehouse, we'd eliminate most of the supply risk.

**B:** Buffer stock is feasible, but I'd need a volume commitment to justify the warehousing cost. If you can guarantee a minimum annual spend of £350,000, I can absorb the storage fees and build the safety stock into our service-level agreement at no extra charge.

**A:** £350,000 is roughly 15% above our current run rate. If we go with the minimum commitment model, I'd expect a volume discount to match — say, a tiered rebate structure: 2% at £300K, 3% at £400K, and 5% at £500K and above.

**B:** That rebate structure is aggressive, David. Our margins on printed packaging are already thin. How about a flat 3% rebate on all spend above £300,000, with a quarterly reconciliation and credit note issued within 30 days? That keeps it simple and predictable for both sides.

**A:** I can work with quarterly reconciliation. Let me also raise the payment terms — we've been on net-30 historically. Given the volume commitment, I'd like to shift to net-60, which is more aligned with our accounts payable cycle.

**B:** Net-60 is a stretch for us on a single contract, but for a three-year framework agreement with the £350K minimum and the rebate structure, I can get it approved. I'll need your signature on a letter of intent by the end of this month to lock in these terms.

**A:** Three-year framework with an annual review clause — I'm comfortable with that. Draw up the draft agreement and I'll have our legal team review it within the week. If there are no deal-breakers, we can finalise by the 30th.

**B:** Excellent. I'll have the draft in your hands by Wednesday. David, I genuinely believe this framework will take the friction out of our supply relationship and let both teams focus on what they do best.

**A:** Agreed, Rachel. Let's make it happen.

## Key Vocabulary

- **lead time** — 交付周期/前置时间 | 从下单到交货的总时长，供应链管理核心术语
- **just-in-time (JIT)** — 准时制生产 | 以最小库存维持生产的精益管理策略
- **penalty clause** — 罚则条款 | 合同中规定违约方需支付赔偿的条款
- **liquidated damages** — 违约金/预定损害赔偿 | 预先约定的违约赔偿金额
- **safety stock** — 安全库存 | 为应对供应波动而持有的额外库存
- **service-level agreement (SLA)** — 服务水平协议 | 约定供应商服务质量标准的正式协议
- **volume discount** — 批量折扣 | 基于采购量的价格优惠
- **tiered rebate** — 阶梯返利 | 不同采购量级对应不同返利比例的结构
- **letter of intent (LOI)** — 意向书 | 正式合同签署前的意向确认文件
- **framework agreement** — 框架协议 | 设定长期合作基本条款的总合同

## Sentence Patterns

- **I won't make excuses — ...** → 我不找借口——…… | 承认问题后立即转向解决方案的坦诚表达
- **I need a firm commitment on...** → 我需要就……做出明确承诺 | 谈判中要求对方给出具体保证的强势句式
- **That's a start, but I'm more interested in...** → 这是个开始，但我更关心…… | 部分认可后引出更深层需求的递进表达
- **I can work with...** → 我可以接受…… | 谈判中表达同意的弹性句式，介于完全接受和拒绝之间
- **If there are no deal-breakers...** → 如果没有致命障碍的话…… | 在推进合作时保留退路的谨慎表达
- **Let's make it happen.** → 让我们把它落实吧 | 谈判收尾时表达积极行动意愿的常用结语

## Chinese Translation

A: Rachel，感谢你过来。我们一直在审视包装供应链，坦率说，过去两个季度你们交付周期的波动给我们的生产车间带来了不少排程上的麻烦。

B: 感谢你直接提出这个问题，David。我不找借口——第一季度的延迟是由我们上游供应商的原材料短缺引起的。不过，我们已经将采购来源多元化，增加了两家备用工厂，最近六周的准时交付率已回升至94%。

A: 这令人鼓舞，但94%仍有差距。我们的生产采用准时制模式——任何超过48小时的延迟都会导致生产线停产。我需要你们就48小时交付窗口做出明确承诺，并写入违约罚则。

B: 我可以就标准SKU承诺48小时窗口，但定制印刷的纸箱由于需要设置印刷机，需要三个工作日。至于罚则——我们的标准合同将违约金上限设为发票金额的5%。对于你这样的大批量客户，我可以提高至8%，这是我方法务团队能接受的上限了。

A: 8%是个开始，但我更关心的是防止违约而非收回赔偿。考虑一下持有缓冲库存怎么样？如果在你们的仓库为我们前二十大SKU保持两周的安全库存，我们可以消除大部分供应风险。

B: 缓冲库存是可行的，但我需要批量承诺来证明仓储成本的合理性。如果你能保证最低年采购额35万英镑，我可以自行承担仓储费用，并将安全库存纳入服务水平协议，不额外收费。

A: 35万英镑大约比我们目前的年化采购额高出15%。如果采用最低承诺模式，我期望同时获得批量折扣——比如阶梯返利结构：30万英镑返2%、40万英镑返3%、50万英镑及以上返5%。

B: David，这个返利结构相当激进。我们在印刷包装上的利润率已经很薄了。不如这样：超过30万英镑的所有采购享受3%的统一返利，按季度结算，30天内出具信用票据？这对双方来说都简洁且可预测。

A: 按季度结算我可以接受。我再提一个付款条件的问题——我们历史上一直是货到30天付款。考虑到量级承诺，我想改为货到60天，这样更符合我们的应付账款周期。

B: 对单个合同来说货到60天有点吃力，但对于一个为期三年、有35万英镑最低承诺和返利结构的框架协议，我可以争取批准。我需要你月底前签署意向书以锁定这些条款。

A: 三年框架加年度审阅条款——我可以接受。起草协议后，我方法务团队一周内完成审核。如果没有致命障碍，30号前可以敲定。

B: 很好。周三前将草稿送到你手上。David，我真心相信这个框架将消除我们供应关系中的摩擦，让双方团队都能专注于各自最擅长的工作。

A: 同意，Rachel。让我们把它落实吧。

## Learning Tip

供应商谈判（Vendor Negotiation）是BEC写作Part 2（提案/报告）和口语Part 3的核心场景。建议重点掌握谈判中的"让步梯度"——从opening position（初始立场）到fallback position（让步后立场），再到walk-away point（底线）。练习时注意区分hard negotiation（强调罚则、排他性）和collaborative negotiation（强调长期合作、双赢）的语言风格差异，考官会考察你根据上下文切换语域的能力。
