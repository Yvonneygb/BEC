# BEC Daily | Franchise Negotiation

## Dialogue

**A (Linda Wang — Franchisee Candidate, Spark Education Group)**

**B (James Hart — Franchise Development Director, BrightStar Learning)**

**A:** James, thank you for walking me through the franchise disclosure document. I'm genuinely excited about the BrightStar brand, but I need to dig into the numbers before I can commit. Your initial franchise fee of £35,000 — is there any room for negotiation there?

**B:** I'm glad you're doing your due diligence, Linda. The initial franchise fee is standardised across all our territories — it covers site selection support, two weeks of intensive training at our headquarters, and the full launch marketing package. That said, we do offer a phased payment option: 60% upfront and 40% within the first six months of operation.

**A:** That payment structure definitely eases the upfront burden. But let's talk about the ongoing royalty — 8% of gross revenue feels steep, especially in the first two years when we're still building the customer base. Most education franchises I've benchmarked sit at 5% to 6%.

**B:** Fair observation, but those benchmarks rarely include the level of ongoing support we provide. Our 8% royalty covers curriculum updates every quarter, a dedicated business development manager who visits monthly, and access to our proprietary learning management system. If you break it down, you're getting a full operational backbone — not just a brand name.

**A:** I do see the value in the support infrastructure. Here's what I'd like to propose: a reduced royalty of 6% for the first 24 months, stepping up to 8% once we hit £250,000 in annual revenue. That gives us the breathing room to reach break-even while protecting your long-term revenue stream.

**B:** That's a creative structure, Linda. I've seen similar arrangements work well. I can't go as low as 6% — our cost to serve a franchisee, including the support team allocation, is around 5.5%. But I can offer 7% for the first 18 months with a revenue trigger at £200,000. That's a compromise that keeps both sides viable.

**A:** 7% for 18 months with a £200,000 trigger — I can work with that. One more thing, though. The territory rights in the draft only cover a three-mile radius. Given that we're targeting suburban areas with spread-out populations, I'd feel more confident with a five-mile exclusivity zone.

**B:** Territory is always a delicate balance. If I expand your radius to five miles, that potentially cannibalises an adjacent territory we've earmarked for a future franchisee. What if I grant you the five miles but with a first-right-of-refusal clause — if we develop the adjacent area within three years, you get priority to take it on?

**A:** That's actually an elegant solution — it protects your growth pipeline while giving me the coverage I need. I'd accept that. So to summarise: £35,000 franchise fee on a 60/40 split, 7% royalty for 18 months stepping to 8% after £200,000 revenue, and a five-mile territory with first right of refusal.

**B:** That captures everything. I'll have the revised franchise agreement drafted by Friday. I appreciate the collaborative approach, Linda — franchise relationships that start with this kind of open negotiation tend to be the most successful ones long-term.

**A:** I couldn't agree more. A franchise is a partnership, not just a transaction. I'm looking forward to reviewing the updated agreement and hopefully signing by the end of the month.

## Key Vocabulary

- **franchise disclosure document (FDD)** — 特许经营披露文件 | 法律要求的核心文件，列出特许经营的所有关键信息
- **initial franchise fee** — 初始加盟费 | 获得品牌使用权和初始支持的一次性费用
- **royalty** — 特许权使用费/提成 | 加盟商定期支付给特许方的收入分成
- **phased payment** — 分期付款 | 将大额费用分阶段支付的灵活安排
- **gross revenue** — 总收入/毛收入 | 未扣减任何成本之前的全部营收
- **break-even** — 盈亏平衡点 | 收入刚好覆盖所有成本的节点
- **territory rights** — 区域经营权 | 加盟商享有排他性经营的地理范围
- **first right of refusal** — 优先购买权/优先权 | 法律条款，给予现有加盟商优先获取相邻区域的权利
- **revenue trigger** — 收入触发点 | 业绩达到某一标准后自动调整条款的机制

## Sentence Patterns

- **I need to dig into the numbers before I can commit.** → 在我做出承诺之前，我需要深入研究数据 | 表达审慎态度，购买/投资决策前的标准商务表达
- **That said, we do offer...** → 话虽如此，我们确实提供…… | 在对对方观点表示认同后提出替代方案或让步
- **Fair observation, but those benchmarks rarely include...** → 合理的观察，但那些行业基准很少包含…… | 承认对方观点的同时提供新的视角信息
- **That gives us the breathing room to reach break-even.** → 这给了我们足够的缓冲空间以达到盈亏平衡 | 表达需要发展空间和时间弹性
- **If I expand your radius to five miles, that potentially cannibalises...** → 如果扩大半径到五英里，可能会侵蚀…… | 委婉指出对方请求可能带来的负面影响
- **A franchise is a partnership, not just a transaction.** → 特许经营是一种合作关系，而不仅仅是一笔交易 | 建立长期合作关系的愿景表达

## Chinese Translation

A: James，感谢你带我走了一遍特许经营披露文件。我对 BrightStar 品牌真的很感兴趣，但在承诺之前需要深入了解数据。35000英镑的初始加盟费——这个还有协商空间吗？

B: 很高兴看到你在做尽职调查，Linda。初始加盟费在所有区域都是标准化的——它涵盖了选址支持、总部两周强化培训以及全套开业营销方案。不过，我们确实提供分期付款的选择：60%预付，40%在运营的前六个月付清。

A: 那个付款结构确实减轻了前期负担。但让我们谈谈持续的特许权使用费——8%的总收入提成，尤其是在前两年我们还在建立客户基础的阶段，感觉偏高。我对比过的大多数教育类特许经营都在5%到6%之间。

B: 合理的观察，但那些行业基准很少包含我们提供的持续支持力度。我们的8%提成涵盖了每季度更新的课程体系、每月到访的专属业务发展经理，还有我们自研的学习管理系统的使用权限。如果拆分来看，你得到的是一个完整的运营后台——而不仅仅是一个品牌名。

A: 我确实看到了支持体系的价值。我这样提议：前24个月的特许权使用费降低到6%，当年收入达到25万英镑后再升至8%。这样给了我们达到盈亏平衡的缓冲空间，同时保护了你们长期的收入流。

B: 这是一个有创意的方案，Linda。我见过类似的安排行得通。我不能降到6%——我们服务每个加盟商的成本（包括支持团队的分摊）大约是5.5%。但我可以提供前18个月7%，收入触发点为20万英镑。这是一个兼顾双方持续经营的折中方案。

A: 前18个月7%，20万英镑触发点——我可以接受。不过还有一件事。草稿中的区域经营权只覆盖三英里半径。考虑到我们瞄准的是人口分散的郊区市场，五英里的排他区域会让我更有信心。

B: 区域划分总是需要微妙的平衡。如果把半径扩大到五英里，可能会侵蚀我们为未来加盟商预留的相邻区域。这样如何：我给你五英里，但附带优先购买权条款——如果三年内我们开发相邻区域，你拥有优先经营权。

A: 这其实是一个很优雅的解决方案——既保护了你们的增长规划，又给了我所需的覆盖范围。我接受。总结一下：35000英镑加盟费按60/40分期支付，前18个月7%特许权使用费，年收入达20万英镑后升至8%，五英里经营区域附带优先购买权。

B: 全部总结到位。我会在周五前准备好修订后的特许经营协议。感谢你的合作方式，Linda——以这种开放式谈判开始的特许经营关系，长期来看往往是最成功的。

A: 完全同意。特许经营是合作关系，而不仅仅是一笔交易。期待审阅更新后的协议，希望能在月底前签约。

## Learning Tip

在BEC考试中，特许经营（Franchise）是商务阅读和听力的常见话题。重点掌握"数字谈判"的表达——如分期付款、收入触发点、阶梯式费率等。建议练习时先熟悉FDD（特许经营披露文件）中的标准条款英文名称，因为这部分词汇专业性强，但在考试中经常作为背景材料出现。口语Part 3中涉及"达成协议"的讨论时，可以运用"phased payment""compromise"等进阶词汇代替简单的"agree"。
